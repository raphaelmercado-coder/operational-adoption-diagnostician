# Hard Mode B: The Failing Route

## Intended outcome

Within three weeks of release, service analysts should process all standard address corrections in Tool K rather than the legacy queue.

## Technical completion

The 2 February release report marks Tool K production-ready. Pre-release integration, permissions, response-time, and recovery tests passed.

## Preparation and ownership

- All 32 analysts completed scenario training.
- The Service Operations Manager accepted operational ownership on 3 February.
- Support coverage and escalation are documented.

## Transition evidence

- The procedure approved on 7 February names Tool K as the official record and limits the legacy queue to declared outages.
- Supervisors measure completion from Tool K.
- The Service Operations Manager confirmed the route at daily briefings.

## Outcome evidence

During the first two days, Tool K received 318 live correction attempts. System records show 121 attempts failed during the final write to the customer database. Analysts opened support incidents and used the authorized outage route for those cases. Attempts fell sharply after repeated failures. The incident register links the failures to a production integration timeout not reproduced in pre-release testing.

## Distractors

Three users disliked the screen layout. Two users asked for more training examples. One supervisor initially used an outdated slide in a briefing and corrected it the same day.

## Test question

Why did Tool K fail to become the sustained operational route for standard address corrections?
