# Hard Mode A: The Parallel Route

## Intended outcome

Within three weeks of release, service analysts should process all standard address corrections in Tool K rather than the legacy queue.

## Technical completion

The 2 February release report marks Tool K production-ready. Integration, permissions, response-time, and recovery tests passed. The incident register records no production failure during the test window.

## Preparation and ownership

- All 32 analysts completed scenario training.
- The Service Operations Manager accepted operational ownership on 3 February.
- Support coverage and escalation are documented.

## Transition evidence

- The procedure approved on 7 February still names the legacy queue as the official record.
- A draft revision names Tool K, but the revision was never approved.
- Analysts were told in training to "use Tool K when appropriate" while supervisors continued measuring completion from the legacy queue.

## Outcome evidence

During the next three weeks, Tool K recorded 41 demonstrations and 12 live corrections. The legacy queue recorded 1,106 live corrections. Interviews show analysts who used Tool K copied each completed case back into the legacy queue so it would count toward team performance.

## Distractors

Three users disliked the screen layout. Two users missed training but later watched the recording. One early notification was delayed.

## Test question

Why did Tool K fail to become the operational route for standard address corrections?
