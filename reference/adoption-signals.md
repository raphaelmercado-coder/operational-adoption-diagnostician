# Operational Adoption Signals

This file defines the outcome being diagnosed. These are evaluation signals, not universal targets and not remediation recommendations.

## Technical completion

A release is technically complete when the responsible technical function represents the agreed technical scope as built, tested to its stated standard, and available in the intended environment.

Technical completion does not by itself establish that:

- the business outcome is defined or achieved
- users can incorporate the capability into live work
- access, support, governance, and exceptions are operational
- an authorized owner accepted accountability
- adoption is sustained

## Operational adoption

A capability is operationally adopted when the intended people use it through the intended operating route with enough reliability and continuity to produce or support the intended business behavior.

Evidence should connect four elements:

1. Actor: the intended role or population
2. Behavior: the work they are expected to perform differently
3. Route: the live process in which the capability is used
4. Outcome: the operational result or control the behavior supports

Activity without these links may be exposure, trial, or isolated use rather than adoption.

## Observable failure patterns

### Activation failure

The intended population never begins using the capability in live work after technical completion.

### Sustained-use failure

Use begins but drops below the defined operating expectation or reverts to the prior route.

### Coverage failure

Use persists only in a limited team, location, case type, or individual and does not reach the intended scope.

### Workflow-displacement failure

The old process, workaround, or parallel system remains the operative route despite the release.

### Outcome-conversion failure

Use occurs, but the expected operating behavior or result does not follow.

The case should identify which pattern occurred. "Low adoption" alone is too vague.

## Evidence of use

Stronger indicators include:

- completed workflow events attributable to intended users
- replacement or retirement of the former route
- repeated use across the relevant time window
- handled exceptions and support events
- operating outcomes tied to the released capability

Weaker indicators include:

- attendance at demonstrations or training
- access provision
- logins without workflow completion
- positive feedback
- release announcements
- technical deployment status

Weak indicators may support context, but they do not independently establish adoption.

## Setting a case-specific threshold

The evidence packet should define the expected actor, behavior, route, outcome, and time window. A useful threshold is specific enough that two informed readers would agree whether the outcome occurred.

Examples:

- Weak: "The team should adopt the tool quickly."
- Observable: "Within four weeks of release, regional coordinators should process routine requests through the new workflow rather than the shared spreadsheet."

Do not invent a threshold after seeing the result. If none was defined contemporaneously, state the limitation and use only outcome claims supported by the record.

## Boundary cases

- If the capability was never represented as technically complete, the case is premature for this diagnostician.
- If use occurred but the software repeatedly failed during real work, technical-operability failure may be primary if the evidence supports it.
- If use is healthy but business performance did not improve, the failure may concern product value or strategy rather than operational adoption.
- If no valid observation establishes non-adoption, withhold diagnosis rather than diagnosing the measurement gap as though non-adoption were proven.
