# VA EHR Operational Adoption Evidence Packet

## Blind-test question

Diagnose why Mann-Grandstaff clinical and administrative staff were not able to use the new electronic health record's core functions reliably after implementation.

## Evidence boundary

This is an evidence-only reconstruction from dated records, attributed accounts, training artifacts, survey results, and operating data reproduced or described in VA OIG Report 20-01930-183. The OIG's findings, conclusions, causal labels, and recommendations are deliberately excluded.

The full OIG report is the held-out validation source. Do not consult it while diagnosing this packet.

Statements below identify whether they are a record, attributed account, or analyst limitation. Page references use the report's printed page numbers.

## Artifact 1 - Deployment and operating outcome record

**Record**

- VA originally targeted March 28, 2020, for the first deployment at Mann-Grandstaff.
- On February 10, 2020, VA postponed the planned deployment when the system was described as 75-80 percent ready.
- VA resumed implementation activity in August 2020. During the intervening period, the program reported completing infrastructure-readiness work and the design, build, connectivity, and technical testing required for 73 interfaces.
- On September 30, 2020, the program executive told Congress that remaining key milestones would be completed before deployment.
- On October 24, 2020, clinical and administrative staff began using the system for live work.

**Post-deployment observation**

- Two to three months after deployment, an OIG survey asked staff about four core functions: finding relevant patient information, sharing patient information, navigating applications, and documenting patient care.
- Only 5 percent of applicable respondents answered affirmatively on all four functions.

**Source:** VA OIG 20-01930-183, pp. 5, 9, 11, and 13.

## Artifact 2 - Formal responsibilities and training approval record

**Record**

- The VA Office of Electronic Health Record Modernization (OEHRM) was responsible for implementation of the new system, including training.
- Veterans Health Administration clinical and administrative personnel were the operational users expected to deliver care and perform daily work through the system.
- Cerner developed the training plans and materials. VA National Councils reviewed instructor-led content for alignment with validated workflow decisions.
- VA OEHRM then reviewed and approved the training materials and was responsible for checking alignment with training objectives, workflow, functionality, and audience.
- The training strategy made course completion and proficiency checks prerequisites for system access.

**Source:** VA OIG 20-01930-183, pp. 1, 5-6, and 10.

## Artifact 3 - Operational-readiness activity record

**December 2019 memorandum**

- The Facility Director told VA OEHRM's Executive Director and Chief Medical Officer that the scheduled mock go-live would not provide a comprehensive real-world operational assessment.
- He requested at least three weeks of scenario-based assessment by facility staff to identify functional obstacles before deployment.

**January 8-9, 2020 meeting and follow-up**

- The Deputy Secretary convened VA, VHA, and VA OEHRM leaders.
- The Acting Under Secretary for Health wrote that the planned testing would not fully assess the system's capabilities, noted that some manual workarounds were more significant than anticipated, and endorsed a full operational assessment before deployment.
- He characterized the assessment as necessary for patient safety.

**Scheduling outcome**

- The operational assessment was placed on the schedule before facility staff had completed system training.
- The Facility Director decided it would not be useful to run the assessment with untrained staff.
- The requested assessment was not completed before the planned March 2020 deployment date.

**Source:** VA OIG 20-01930-183, pp. 37-38.

## Artifact 4 - Super-user feedback and release-response record

**February 3, 2020**

- The Facility Director met with more than 30 super users and reported broadly negative feedback about the training.
- A clinical council co-chair separately wrote to senior VA OEHRM leaders that the training had left participants demoralized, distrustful, and less prepared to assist other users.

**February 5, 2020**

- VA OEHRM's Chief Medical Officer and the Facility Director met with 50 super users.
- The Chief Medical Officer also considered a post-training survey completed by 22 of the 81 super users who had finished training. Those respondents generally rated the training favorably.
- The 22 survey respondents represented 27 percent of the 81 trained super users.
- After considering survey results and observations from change-management personnel, the Chief Medical Officer and VA OEHRM Executive Director decided the training issues did not require delaying end-user training or deployment.

**February 6-10, 2020**

- The Facility Director told the Acting Under Secretary for Health that significant readiness concerns remained and recommended a delay of at least 90 days.
- Four days later, VA announced that deployment would be postponed.

**Evidence limitation**

- A contemporaneous performance assessment stated that training-attendance and completion data were delayed and could not be validated using VA systems, reducing confidence in the reporting available to leadership.

**Source:** VA OIG 20-01930-183, pp. 38-39.

## Artifact 5 - Training design, workflow, and practice-environment record

**Training plan**

- The new system introduced more than 900 new workflows for facility users.
- The approved strategy required the 300-level instructor-led curriculum to incorporate workflow and process changes.
- Course sequences included software instruction, scenario-based activities, supplemental resources, and proficiency checks.

**Attributed operational-user accounts**

- Facility personnel described classroom instruction as concentrating on how to execute software actions without enough context for how the actions fit into daily clinical and administrative work.
- Personnel reported that practice-based questions were often deferred and not resolved before deployment.
- Some topics were moved to more than 200 job aids, tip sheets, and reference materials that were optional rather than required coursework.

**Practice environment**

- The training environment was finalized in May 2020, while production functionality continued to change afterward.
- Some production workflows and user roles were absent from the training environment.
- Access was restricted to scheduled classroom activities; general practice access was not widely available.

**Role assignment**

- Training assignments depended on mapping staff into more than 300 system roles.
- Facility personnel reported incorrect role assignments, multiple-role complications, staffing changes, limited instructions, and repeated scheduling corrections.

**Source:** VA OIG 20-01930-183, pp. 6, 10-20.

## Artifact 6 - Post-deployment survey and operating-data record

**Survey method**

- The OIG invited 1,799 facility employees to participate two to three months after deployment.
- 809 employees responded, a 45 percent response rate.
- 722 respondents used the new system or required access to it for their work.

**Core-function responses**

- 62 percent disagreed or strongly disagreed that relevant patient information was readily available.
- 53 percent disagreed or strongly disagreed that they could share patient information with other clinicians without difficulty.
- 65 percent disagreed or strongly disagreed that they could navigate the system's applications without difficulty.
- 55 percent disagreed or strongly disagreed that they could document patient care without difficulty.
- Five percent answered affirmatively on all four functions.

**Completed-appointment volumes**

Compared with the same months in 2019, completed appointments were lower by:

- 30.7 percent in August 2020,
- 25.6 percent in September 2020,
- 42.1 percent in October 2020, and
- 41.5 percent in November 2020.

**Limitations**

- COVID-19 contributed to reduced appointment volume, so the operating data cannot attribute the entire decline to the system transition.
- The survey response rate was 45 percent and does not establish the experience of every employee.
- This packet does not establish whether software defects independently caused part of the operational failure.

**Source:** VA OIG 20-01930-183, pp. 9, 11, 13, and 40-41.
