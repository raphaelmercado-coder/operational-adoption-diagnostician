## Model response

### FAILURE OBSERVED

After the new EHR went live on October 24, 2020, Mann-Grandstaff clinical and administrative staff used it but could not incorporate its core functions reliably into daily work. Two to three months later, only 5 percent of OIG survey respondents reported being able to perform all four assessed core functions readily.

### PRIMARY CAUSE

The implementation authority controlled training approval and go-live while VHA’s operational users lacked an effective role in those decisions, allowing workflow and readiness evidence to be overridden instead of incorporated into preparation for live clinical work.

### CAUSAL CHAIN

VA OEHRM held implementation and training authority, while the governance structure gave VHA no defined decision-making or oversight role despite VHA staff being the operational users.

VHA leaders requested a real-world readiness assessment, but it was scheduled before staff training and never conducted; later, widespread negative super-user feedback was discounted in the go-live decision.

Without operational evidence carrying decision force, approved training emphasized software actions rather than how more than 900 new workflows operated in day-to-day clinical work.

Users consequently entered production with incomplete workflow understanding, incorrect role-based training in some cases, a mismatched practice environment, and limited effective support.

After two to three months of use, most surveyed users still reported difficulty navigating the system, documenting care, accessing patient information, or sharing it with clinicians.

### DECISIVE EVIDENCE

The VA OIG report `VA-EHR-training-deficiencies-case.pdf` establishes that:

In December 2019 and January 2020, the Facility Director and Acting Under Secretary for Health requested a scenario-based operational assessment before go-live; it did not occur.

On February 3–5, 2020, more than 30 and then 50 super users communicated serious training concerns. VA OEHRM instead emphasized favorable results from 22 respondents—27 percent of the 81 super users then trained—and concluded the concerns did not require delaying training and go-live.

VA OEHRM’s Director of Change Management acknowledged that the program had managed the technology but missed how it fit into daily processes.

Of 1,799 staff invited to the post-go-live OIG survey, 809 responded, including 722 who used or required EHR access. Only 5 percent agreed or strongly agreed with all four core-usability statements.

### WHY THIS IS THE CAUSE, NOT A SYMPTOM

“Button-ology,” missing workflow content, role-assignment errors, and weak support describe how operational preparation failed. The governance mechanism explains why these different deficiencies survived repeated warnings and reached go-live together.

If VHA’s operational evidence had carried effective authority, the readiness assessment and severe super-user feedback would have required an operational judgment before release. The exact result is inferential, but the documented deficiencies could not have been dismissed through the same decision process.

### STRONGEST ALTERNATIVE RULED OUT

Deficient training content is the closest alternative. It directly contributed to users’ difficulties, but it is a downstream mechanism rather than the primary organizational cause: VA OEHRM approved the curriculum, knew of recurring contractor deficiencies, received explicit workflow and readiness concerns, and still lacked a process through which VHA could make those concerns determinative.

### CONFIDENCE AND LIMITS

**Moderate.** Contemporaneous memoranda, governance records, attributed accounts, training artifacts, and the post-go-live survey support the chain. The conclusion is limited by the report’s training-focused scope, the survey’s 45 percent response rate, altered or withheld VA OEHRM evaluation data, and incomplete technical-operability evidence. Software functionality problems and COVID-19 may have contributed, but the supplied packet does not establish either as a stronger primary cause.
