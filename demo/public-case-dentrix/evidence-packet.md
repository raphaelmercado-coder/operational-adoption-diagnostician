# Evidence Packet: MHS GENESIS Dentrix

## Case boundary

This case concerns the Dentrix dental module inside the Department of Defense's MHS GENESIS electronic health record. It asks why the module failed to become reliably operational after deployment of the broader EHR. It does not attempt to diagnose the entire MHS GENESIS program or the separate question of system-wide user satisfaction.

## Intended operational outcome

DOD implemented MHS GENESIS to replace multiple legacy health-record systems with a comprehensive, real-time EHR. Dentrix was the included dental module. Its critical interface was intended to support shared dental and medical record functionality for clinicians treating service members and other DOD beneficiaries.

Source: GAO-24-106187, pages 2-3 and 19-21.

## Technical completion and deployment condition

DOD began deploying MHS GENESIS in 2017. GAO reported that DOD completed the final deployment of the broader system in March 2024 and had deployed it at all of its medical facilities.

The dental module was part of that deployed EHR, but the module had a separately tracked operational issue.

Source: GAO-24-106187, highlights and pages 2-3, 19-21.

## Operational failure evidence

Dentrix problems began as early as October 2018. During September 2019 deployments, users experienced excessive delays. The interface between Dentrix and other EHR components was turned off to improve stability, but disabling it left the module minimally functional because the interface provided critical capability.

The program tracked the issue in monthly management meetings from at least July 2019. A roadmap intended to deliver fixes by June 2020 was unsuccessful. The issue was elevated to significant in March 2021 and severe in July 2023.

As of January 2024, DOD stated that the vendor would not be able to provide the interface required for critical functionality. GAO concluded that the issue remained unresolved and that MHS GENESIS would not provide critical functionality to dentists until an alternative approach was identified.

Source: GAO-24-106187, pages 19-21.

## Mechanism evidence

The program office attributed the persistent problem to the module's inability to support an increased number of dental users. It reported that every Dentrix release failed scalability testing when the critical interface was enabled.

The program office also reported systemic vendor difficulty delivering fundamental capability on schedule and budget, along with software-development and quality-assurance practices that allowed defects and errors.

Source: GAO-24-106187, pages 19-20.

## Potentially competing evidence

At the joint DOD-VA Federal Health Care Center, VA determined that Dentrix did not meet its dental requirements and could not integrate with VA's legacy dental system. The facility therefore planned for both systems to be used, including two computers at each workstation.

This evidence may support a workflow-fit explanation for the VA-specific deployment. The diagnostician must determine whether it explains the broader Dentrix operational failure better than the documented scalability and interface mechanism.

Source: GAO-24-106187, pages 10-12.

## Evidence limitations

- The packet is based on GAO's audit record, not raw vendor source code or complete incident logs.
- It does not establish how every dental user behaved at every facility.
- The broader EHR was deployed, but the supplied record does not characterize Dentrix itself as successfully completed to its intended operational standard.
- GAO's report contains recommendations, but they are excluded from the diagnostic task because this folder diagnoses causes rather than prescribing remedies.

## Test question

Why did the Dentrix dental module fail to become reliably operational even after DOD completed deployment of the broader MHS GENESIS electronic health record?
